PERCEPTION → GENERATION

OPERA

Object Perception Enhances Single-view 3D Reconstruction

We propose OPERA, a unified perceptual alignment framework that turns pretrained perceptual knowledge into explicit guidance for instance-faithful 3D generation. Lightweight alignment modules map intermediate generative representations to perceptual representations, allowing discrepancies with input-derived targets to guide the sampling trajectory. This principle supports gradient-based denoising guidance for multiview diffusion and velocity guidance for native 3D flow matching, while keeping both generative backbones and perception models frozen.

01 / MOTIVATION

Plausible is not the same as faithful.

A single photograph leaves most of an object unseen. Generative models fill the gap with convincing detail, but convincing detail can belong to some object of the category rather than to the one in the image. Instance fidelity asks for the particular object: its proportions, surface patterns and distinctive parts. The figure shows one example per paradigm, a multiview diffusion model and a native 3D flow-matching model.

02 / OUR APPROACH

Let perception guide generation.

One alignment principle, instantiated for two generative paradigms.

01

Frozen backbone, frozen perception

The generator and the perception models keep their weights. Their operations stay differentiable, so gradients can reach the sampling state.

02

Lightweight GPAM

A small alignment module per perceptual target is the only thing trained, on about a thousand Objaverse objects, independently of the other modules.

03

Sampling-time guidance

The target is computed once from the input image. At guided steps the state is corrected along the alignment gradient, and the sampler continues from the corrected state.

04 / COMPONENTS

What each signal contributes.

Geometry and appearance are guided by different signals, alone or together.

Structure guidance changes geometry: it acts on the sparse layout from which the shape is then generated. Material guidance changes appearance only, on fixed geometry. Combined uses the structure-guided geometry and then guides its material.

Material guidance needs a camera fitted to the generated shape. When the fitted silhouette overlaps the input by less than 0.8 IoU, guidance is switched off for that object and the output is the unguided fallback. Such objects stay in the reported cohorts and are labelled in the gallery; they are not counted as guided results.

Depth guidance carries geometric structure; semantic guidance carries what the object is and how its surface looks. Each is trained separately and they are mixed at sampling time. On the two benchmarks they help differently: depth alone gives most of the geometric gain on GSO, while the semantic signal matters more on OmniObject3D.

External methods: ReconViaGen and Hi3DGen, run from their official releases.
